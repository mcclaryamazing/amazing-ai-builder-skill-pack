## Universal Persuasion Model

Start with a hungry market and a strong offer. Copy can amplify demand, clarify desire, reduce risk, and make the next step feel obvious, but it cannot rescue a weak product/offer mismatch.

Use this order:

1. Audience: who is this for?
2. Pain/desire: what do they already want fixed, gained, avoided, or felt?
3. Product category: what is the concrete solution?
4. Core benefit: what changes in their life?
5. Mechanism: why should they believe this product can do it?
6. Proof: what evidence supports the promise?
7. Offer: why act now or here?
8. Risk reversal: what makes the action feel safe?
9. CTA: what exact next step should they take?

Copy formulas to adapt:

- Problem -> product category -> outcome -> proof -> CTA.
- Feature -> "so you can" -> practical benefit -> emotional payoff.
- Before state -> mechanism -> after state.
- Objection -> reassurance -> proof/risk reversal -> CTA.
- Audience + problem + product category + primary outcome.

## Landing Page Copy

Landing pages for paid traffic need one primary conversion goal and strong message match.

Hero:

- Confirm the visitor is in the right place.
- Echo the driving ad's problem/desire, offer, and emotional frame.
- Use a short benefit-driven headline.
- Use the subhead to name product category, audience, mechanism, or secondary benefit.
- Put CTA above the fold when the offer is direct.
- Add nearby microcopy for risk reversal, shipping, guarantee, quiz length, or discount.

Section sequence patterns:

- Direct PDP: hero -> proof bar -> benefits -> product mechanism -> reviews -> offer/pricing -> guarantee -> FAQ -> final CTA.
- Quiz funnel: hero -> quiz promise -> why personalize -> proof -> first question -> benefits -> offer/risk reversal -> FAQ -> sticky CTA.
- Subscription: hero -> routine promise -> value props -> how subscription works -> savings/value math -> reviews -> guarantee -> FAQ -> start plan CTA.
- Advertorial/listicle: problem story -> surprising insight -> product bridge -> proof -> offer -> FAQ -> CTA.

Section copy rules:

- Write H2s as benefit statements, not labels.
- Keep body copy short and mobile-readable.
- Make bullets feature-benefit pairs.
- Put proof close to the claim it supports.
- Use FAQ to answer practical objections: fit, usage, shipping, returns, cancellation, safety, ingredients/materials, compatibility, timing.
- Use final CTA copy that restates the core outcome and offer.

CTA rules:

- Use clear command verbs: `Shop Now`, `Add to Cart`, `Take the Quiz`, `Start Your Plan`, `Claim My Discount`.
- Match the CTA to the real destination and offer.
- Repeat CTA after major persuasion blocks.
- Surround CTA with true friction reducers: guarantee, free shipping, secure checkout, delivery speed, easy returns, subscription cancellation.

## Meta Ad Copy And Ad-To-LP Match

Meta copy should be retrieval-safe first and persuasive second.

Primary text structure:

1. First 1-2 sentences: problem/desire + product category + core outcome.
2. Next 1-2 sentences: benefit expansion and emotional payoff.
3. Next 1-3 sentences: proof, scenario, or short story.
4. Final line: risk reversal and CTA.

First 200 characters:

- Name the problem/desire.
- Name the product category.
- Include a concrete benefit.
- Avoid metaphor-only openings.
- Use plain terms a platform can classify.

Headline:

- Product category + primary benefit.
- Short, clear, and aligned with primary text.
- Use specificity when true: audience, timeframe, guarantee, offer.

Description:

- Reinforce benefit + proof.
- Keep it brief.
- Repeat the same semantic cluster naturally.

Landing page match:

- Hero headline should continue the ad's angle.
- Hero subhead should clarify the same product/category/outcome.
- Do not switch from one selling angle to another after the click.
- If the ad leads with a problem, the LP must address that problem immediately.

## Product Page Copy

PDP copy should both inform and persuade.

Recommended order:

1. Product title: product name + clear category/use case.
2. Benefit tagline.
3. Short intro: core outcome and who it is for.
4. Benefit bullets: each feature tied to a result.
5. Usage/mechanism: how it works or how to use it.
6. Specs: materials, dimensions, ingredients, compatibility, care, sizing.
7. Proof: review snippets, ratings, certifications, studies, founder/expert credibility.
8. Offer/trust: price, shipping, guarantee, returns, bundle discount.
9. CTA and optional upsell.

Bullet pattern:

- `Feature name: what it does for the customer.`
- Keep claims source-bound. If a claim needs evidence, flag it.

Upsell/AOV copy:

- Explain complementarity, not just "buy more."
- Tie bundle to a use case: spare, routine, gift, refill, complete set.
- Mention free-shipping thresholds truthfully if supplied.

## Email Copy

Promotional emails:

- Subject line leads with offer, benefit, or true urgency.
- Open quickly with the reason to care.
- Tie the discount/launch to product benefits.
- Use one primary CTA.
- Add AOV nudges like bundles or free-shipping thresholds when true.

Welcome series:

- Email 1: deliver promised incentive, welcome, set expectations, CTA.
- Email 2: brand story, mission, founder/customer context, soft CTA.
- Email 3: best sellers, proof, offer reminder, CTA.

Abandoned cart:

- Send a simple reminder first.
- Bring the product and benefit back into focus.
- Add friction reducers: sizing, shipping, returns, guarantee, support.
- Later reminders may use true urgency or an incentive if allowed.

Content/newsletter:

- Lead with useful content.
- Integrate product as a helpful solution, not a hard sell.
- End with a soft next step.

## Brand Voice

If voice is supplied, obey it. If missing, infer a temporary voice from audience, category, price point, and risk level.

Define:

- 3-6 tone words.
- Do rules.
- Don't rules.
- Preferred and forbidden terminology.
- Messaging pillars.
- Example lines.

Voice should be consistent across channels, but adjust intensity by context. Serious objections, compliance topics, customer support, and medical/safety concerns require more measured language.

## Copy QA Checklist

Use this before finalizing:

- Is there one primary audience and one primary offer?
- Does the first visible copy state a clear outcome?
- Does the hero match the ad or traffic source?
- Does every feature become a benefit?
- Are emotional promises grounded in real product facts?
- Are all claims, stats, testimonials, endorsements, certifications, guarantees, prices, shipping terms, and specs sourced from intake?
- Is proof specific and placed near related claims?
- Are objections answered before the final CTA?
- Is CTA copy clear, action-oriented, and matched to the destination?
- Is urgency true?
- Is the page skimmable on mobile?
- Are missing facts marked with placeholders instead of invented?
