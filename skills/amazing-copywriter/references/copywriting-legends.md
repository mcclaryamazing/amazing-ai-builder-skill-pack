# Copywriting Legends

Use this reference to apply the user's original consumer-brand copywriting method without copying source text or imitating any named writer. Translate the principles into the brand's own voice and keep every claim inside the supplied facts.

## The Combined Method

Strong consumer copy is assembled from research, audience desire, offer strength, a concrete big idea, believable proof, and a clear response path. Use the legends as complementary lenses:

1. Research what is true and what the customer already wants.
2. Find one product-specific big idea that can organize the message.
3. Match the offer and hook to a motivated audience.
4. Give a clear reason why the promise is believable.
5. Build momentum from the first line to the response.
6. Make the transformation emotionally relevant without exaggerating it.
7. Test meaningful alternatives rather than treating any formula as guaranteed.

## Schwartz: Existing Desire And Awareness

Do not try to manufacture desire. Identify the desire, frustration, identity, or job already present in the market, then connect the product to it.

Before writing, identify:

- awareness level: unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, or brand-aware
- dominant desire or urgent job
- language customers use for the problem and desired outcome
- what the audience already believes
- what must become newly believable

Match the amount of explanation to awareness. Cold or problem-aware audiences usually need more context than retargeting, Search, existing-customer, or product-aware audiences.

## Ogilvy: Research, Big Idea, And Brand Character

Extract product facts, differentiators, materials, ingredients, process, founder story, constraints, reviews, objections, offer terms, and proof before writing.

Build one big idea that is:

```text
concrete
product-specific
easy to understand
visual enough to remember
tied to a real product truth
supportable by mechanism, proof, or offer logic
```

Reject empty wordplay and ideas that could fit a competitor unchanged. Treat the customer as intelligent. Preserve the brand's character across headlines, captions, CTAs, FAQs, metadata, and follow-up messages.

## Halbert: Hungry Audience, Offer, And Hook

Order matters:

1. Audience: who already wants the outcome or urgently feels the problem?
2. Offer: what makes action worthwhile and low-friction?
3. Hook: what truthful angle earns continued attention from that audience?

Use curiosity, self-interest, specificity, identity, comparison, pain avoidance, exclusivity, and urgency only when the product and offer can resolve them honestly. Do not use fake scarcity or leave a curiosity loop unresolved.

## Hopkins And Bencivenga: Reason Why And Believability

Every important claim needs a reason to believe. Support it with the most appropriate available evidence:

- human proof: reviews, testimonials, UGC, demonstrations, customer stories
- technical proof: ingredients, materials, specs, testing, process, comparison data
- authority proof: founder expertise, certifications, press, partners, experts
- risk proof: guarantee, warranty, trial, returns, secure checkout
- offer proof: transparent terms, savings math, contents, shipping, cancellation

If proof is missing, use `[PROOF NEEDED: ...]`, `[CLAIM TO VERIFY: ...]`, or softer wording. Never turn an illustrative example into a factual claim.

## Caples: Headline Clarity And Testing

Prioritize a clear benefit, useful news or novelty, truthful curiosity, and easy comprehension. The reader should understand the category, relevance, or promised outcome quickly.

Useful headline shapes:

| Hook | Pattern |
| --- | --- |
| Concrete benefit | `[Specific outcome] without [specific friction]` |
| Mechanism | `The [mechanism or product truth] that helps [audience] [outcome]` |
| Offer | `[Offer] for [audience or use case], with [risk reducer]` |
| Identity | `For [customer identity] who [specific standard or desire]` |
| Comparison | `[Product] vs. [old way]: [decisive difference]` |
| Curiosity | `Why [unexpected product truth] changes [familiar problem]` |

Create materially different alternatives when testing. Do not produce superficial variations that only swap adjectives.

## Sugarman: Reading Momentum

The first sentence earns the second. Each paragraph should answer one question while creating a natural reason to continue.

Maintain momentum with:

- short, concrete openings
- useful specificity
- logical transitions
- open loops that resolve promptly
- stories that lead back to the product truth or offer
- section endings that set up the next belief or action

Remove throat-clearing, repeated claims, and decorative stories that delay comprehension.

## Edwards: Empathy, Transformation, And Response

Use an empathy-led sequence when the audience needs emotional context:

- Person: name the buyer and situation.
- Problem or pain: describe the current friction in plain language.
- Aspiration: show the better state the buyer wants.
- Story or solution: introduce the product, mechanism, founder insight, or process that bridges the gap.
- Transformation and testimony: make the after-state believable with proof.
- Offer: explain what the customer receives and what reduces risk.
- Response: make the next action obvious and low-friction.

Do not shame the customer, exaggerate the before-state, or promise a transformation the evidence cannot support.

## Meta Ad-To-Landing-Page Continuity

Unless the user states otherwise, assume Meta paid-social cold prospecting. The visitor was interrupted in a feed and may not have been shopping for the category. The ad earned attention and the click; the landing page must preserve that momentum and complete the persuasion the ad did not have room to finish.

Identify:

```text
the ad angle and exact promise
why the visitor clicked
what the ad already established
what the visitor still needs to believe
what proof can close that belief gap
what response is appropriate next
```

Choose the persuasion architecture that continues the ad:

- AIDA for aspiration, discovery, launch novelty, or an attention hook that must build desire.
- Problem-solution-proof-offer for a clear pain or job, a credible solution, substantiating proof, and a concrete offer.
- Problem-agitate-solve for truthful problem reframing that does not shame or exaggerate.
- Mechanism-first when the distinctive reason the product works caused the click.
- Demonstration-first when the ad showed an observable result that the page should explain and substantiate.
- Comparison-first when the visitor clicked because of a contrast with the old way or an alternative.

For the default cold Meta case, use problem-solution-proof-offer when no other architecture has a stronger rationale. Do not default to PDP copy merely because the brief names a product.

## Channel Translation

### Meta Ads

- Make the first lines identifiable: problem or desire, product category, and core benefit.
- Use the hook to earn attention, then resolve it with product truth, proof, or offer.
- Keep primary text, headline, description, and destination promise aligned.

### Landing Pages

- Continue the ad's angle in the opening.
- Introduce the product soon enough to preserve trust.
- Put proof near the claims it supports.
- Make the offer, risk reversal, and next action easy to understand.
- Use an education-first sequence for cold traffic unless affirmative purchase intent supports a PDP-style response.

### Product Pages

- Lead with a clear category, use case, and primary outcome.
- Convert features and specifications into practical and emotional benefits.
- Keep selection, price, offer terms, shipping, returns, and purchase controls clear.
- Use proof and mechanism to defend differentiation and price.

### Email

- Give the message one job and one primary CTA.
- Use subject and opening to establish the reason to care quickly.
- Use story only when it naturally leads to the product, offer, or useful insight.
- Keep promotion terms, urgency, and savings truthful.

### Social And Content

- Deliver value, entertainment, or insight before forcing a sale.
- Make product integration relevant to the audience's current problem or desire.
- Preserve the brand voice while keeping claims and endorsements source-bound.

## Copy QA

Before finalizing, confirm:

- one audience, one big idea, and one primary response path are clear
- the copy channels an existing desire rather than inventing one
- the opening is specific to this product, audience, and context
- the offer is understandable without rereading
- the mechanism or reason why makes the promise believable
- every strong claim has proof, a placeholder, or softer wording
- the headline earns attention without sacrificing comprehension
- curiosity resolves truthfully
- the copy has forward momentum without filler
- the transformation is emotionally relevant and evidence-bound
- the CTA matches the actual next action
- the final voice belongs to the brand, not to a named copywriter

Revise any copy that could be pasted onto a competitor's page unchanged.
