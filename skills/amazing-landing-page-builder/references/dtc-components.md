# DTC Components

Write finished, benefit-led customer copy using the product and offer facts the merchant supplies. Use available proof where it strengthens the pitch and show material terms where shoppers need them. Keep internal notes outside the page. Designated image placeholders retain their prescribed production fields.

Use this reference when shaping the section sequence, component responsibilities, CTA system, sticky CTA, comparison table, review summary, cross-sells, email capture, or image slots.

The [source guide index](source-guide-index.md#section-blueprint-lines) points to archived layout and module examples for the mechanism, reviews, cross-sells, and sticky CTA. Those examples are options to adapt, not current mandates or evidence that any product fact or offer term is true.

## Selection Contract

Components are reusable building blocks, not a reason to choose an archetype. For default cold Meta traffic, begin with a transparent listicle opening. Use a commerce-first `HeroBuyBox` opening only when explicitly identified retargeting passes PDP eligibility and PDP clearly wins the last-choice comparison. Specialized destinations do not bypass this gate: use a message-matched narrative hero, then embed necessary selectors or purchase controls at the appropriate persuasive stage. Embedded controls alone do not turn a listicle, subscription funnel or bundle builder into a PDP.

Lead with the customer's benefit and identify the product early. Put an honest next step near the opening and repeat it at meaningful decision points. Show the actual price and purchase details before asking the visitor to compare package value. Put answers to decisive objections where the objections arise, and use an FAQ for any remaining questions. Include a non-buyer exit ramp when useful. On long pages, consider a sticky CTA if it keeps the offer easy to reach without covering content or controls.

## Hero Commerce Stack

For PDP-selected routes, the hero is a compressed buy box, not a billboard. Do not apply this model to the default listicle opening.

Choose the relevant elements:

- product/category or offer eyebrow
- specific outcome or transformation headline
- mechanism plus audience subheadline
- product visual or gallery
- an available proof cue, such as a rating, review, press mention, certification, or customer count, when useful
- offer cue when relevant
- price or selector when relevant
- exact CTA
- a relevant merchant term near the action when it resolves a real hesitation, such as shipping, returns, cancellation, or warranty

For an unfamiliar brand, the most useful product detail, demonstration or merchant reassurance may earn space in the hero. Choose the one that helps the buyer understand or believe the main benefit.

## CTA Matrix

Use:

- `Add to Cart` or `Add to Bag` only when product/variant is resolved
- `Select Size` when size must be selected
- `Choose Flavors` or `Choose a Pack` for consumables or pack selection
- `Build Your Bundle` for required bundles
- `Take the Quiz` or `Find Your Match` for quiz funnels
- `Customize`, `Start Designing`, or `Upload Your Design` for custom goods
- `Try Free` when the merchant offers a free trial
- `Verify Eligibility` for eligibility flows
- `Sign Up`, `Get My Sample`, or `Join the Waitlist` for lead capture
- price-anchored CTAs such as `Add to Cart - $89` for high-AOV or configurable products when accurate

## Component Cards

PromoBar:

- purpose: announce offer, shipping, urgency, or trust
- rules: one clear message, real urgency only, avoid competing bars

Header:

- purpose: brand recognition and minimal navigation
- rules: keep paid landing-page navigation minimal; allow category navigation for collection pages

AdvertorialHeader:

- purpose: establish a transparent brand-owned or sponsored editorial frame
- rules: show brand/sponsor identity, disclose ownership when the styling could imply third-party coverage, and never invent a publication or reporter

MessageMatchedHero:

- purpose: continue the ad hook and make the click feel coherent
- rules: resolve the premise, name and show the product, and offer a clear commercial next step. For a listicle, use a visible counted H1 such as “7 Reasons Why…” and match it to the numbered body headings. An early “See Packages” or “Choose Your Pack” can lead to the actual offer without a buy box. See the [listicle playbook](listicle-playbook.md).

NumberedReasonsSection:

- purpose: build a scannable, cumulative listicle argument
- rules: make the headings tell the sales argument on their own. Give each reason a distinct payoff or objection and develop it through real product details, a recognizable situation, a useful visual or available evidence. Give complex reasons the space they need and keep purposeful reinforcement under `../SKILL.md`. Use the actual number of distinct reasons.

SolutionAndProductReveal:

- purpose: connect the problem or desire to the product and its mechanism
- rules: name the product in the opening. Use a later mechanism section only if it can deepen the argument, with a visual when that helps explain how the product works

ProofNearClaims:

- purpose: make a particular benefit or mechanism easier to believe
- rules: place useful merchant-supplied demonstrations, ratings, reviews or other evidence beside the point they illuminate. Omit a standalone proof module when it would add nothing

OfferAndPurchasePath:

- purpose: make the offer clear and accessible whenever a shopper is ready
- rules: show what each package contains, the quantities, the actual price, any required selections and the exact next-step CTA. Include terms and fulfillment details that affect the decision. Put a real discounted price next to its comparison price and savings. When the comparison is an actual former price, a strike-through makes the difference easy to see. Compare equivalent quantities and label purchased-separately value as such.

RiskReversal:

- purpose: reduce remaining action risk
- rules: use applicable merchant terms, such as returns, guarantee, trial, warranty, cancellation, shipping, compatibility or support, when they resolve a real concern

HeroBuyBox:

- purpose: orient and convert visitors in an eligible, selected PDP opening
- rules: use only after PDP eligibility and destination selection are both satisfied; make the product, actual offer and price understandable, place relevant available evidence where it helps, and match the CTA to the real next step

ProofStrip:

- purpose: reduce bounce and build instant credibility
- rules: use only available, relevant evidence; vary proof types when that helps comprehension, and omit this strip if it adds no value

ValuePropGrid:

- purpose: translate benefits into fast-scannable reasons to believe
- rules: use as many distinct benefits as the product warrants; avoid generic adjectives and tie each item to a buyer friction

MechanismSection:

- purpose: explain why the product works or why it is different
- rules: use when the mechanism closes a real belief gap, especially for premium, technical, ingredient/material, wellness, baby/pet, outdoor, and custom products; choose a split image/text layout, focused cards, a contrasting band, or an annotated visual according to what makes the explanation clearest

IngredientOrMaterialPanel:

- purpose: build trust through transparency
- rules: explain relevant ingredients or materials through their practical benefits; raise only essential unanswered factual questions separately

DimensionOrAnatomyDiagram:

- purpose: visualize dimensions, layers, compatibility, setup, or hidden mechanisms
- rules: use for home, furniture, baby gear, outdoor gear, electronics, wellness tech, and custom products

HowItWorks:

- purpose: reduce process anxiety
- rules: use for subscriptions, quiz funnels, custom products, setup-heavy products, lead capture, and samples

ComparisonTable:

- purpose: defend value or clarify differentiation
- rules: compare named alternatives or a clearly defined category on criteria that matter to the decision. Keep the conditions comparable and distinguish unknown from absent. Introduce the actual price before asking shoppers to compare package value or savings

OfferStack:

- purpose: make the reason to act clear
- rules: explain the actual contents, price and relevant terms of the offer; include an incentive, bundle value, risk reduction, or reason to act only when real and useful

SubscriptionToggle:

- purpose: show one-time vs subscription value honestly
- rules: disclose cadence, first-order price, future price, cancellation terms, and savings math

BundleBuilder:

- purpose: help users build a valid multi-item order
- rules: show progress, selected items, price/savings, and a persistent order summary; group numerous choices into meaningful category tabs when that reduces selection friction, preserve selected state across tabs, and show what remains before the bundle is complete

UGCGallery:

- purpose: make the product feel real and used
- rules: use real UGC only; avoid unsupported before/after claims

ReviewSummary:

- purpose: reduce cognitive load from many reviews
- rules: summarize real review themes and show the supplied count when useful. For apparel, use available fit context such as usual and purchased size or height. For high-AOV products, useful context can include verified-buyer status, usage time, the use case, and authentic photo/video reviews

CrossSellRail:

- purpose: increase AOV or prevent dead ends
- rules: time placement to its purpose: simple add-ons may follow a clear primary offer, routine builders fit after product education, and alternative products can help after reviews or near an exit. Do not distract before the primary product is understood; keep cards priced and actionable

FAQAccordion:

- purpose: handle objections near the end
- rules: answer meaningful remaining purchase questions using the FAQ selection guidance in dtc-visual-copy-system.md; omit this component when it adds nothing

FinalCTA:

- purpose: renew the reason to act and give direct access to the offer before the footer
- rules: reinforce the core outcome and provide a clear purchase action or link to the selector. Repeated purchase access does not require repeated prices; keep them where they help the current decision, such as an offer-led opening or selected-price sticky control.

EmailCapture:

- purpose: create a non-buyer exit ramp
- rules: keep it late unless lead capture is primary; explain what the user receives

StickyCTA:

- purpose: keep conversion access available during long mobile scrolls
- rules: show the product or offer, the actual selected price or variant when useful, and the real next-step CTA. Reserve space for it on small screens and keep content and controls accessible. Keep selection controls in one place unless duplication helps the shopper

## Image Slot Standard

The first wireframe contains placeholders exclusively, even for existing assets. Do not select, generate or insert final imagery until the internally reviewed wireframe passes the approval gate in dtc-design-package.md. Specify ideal image purpose, description, dimensions and crop independently of available assets:

```json
{
  "slot_id": "hero-product-lifestyle",
  "role": "hero image",
  "persuasive_purpose": "Product-specific belief or objection this visual resolves",
  "section": "hero",
  "desktop_placement": "right column, full height of hero content",
  "mobile_placement": "above headline, full-width",
  "aspect_ratio": "4:5",
  "target_dimensions": "1200 x 1500 px",
  "alignment": "center center",
  "mobile_crop": "Preserve product detail inside the narrow-screen safe area",
  "crop_behavior": "cover with product and face/hands inside safe area",
  "description": "Final image description and crop notes",
  "planned_shopify_files_filename": "hero-product-lifestyle.jpg",
  "alt_text": "Descriptive alt text",
  "zoom": true
}
```

Visible placeholder content in the Shopify build should be limited to:

```text
IMAGE: <SLOT-ID>
ASPECT RATIO: <RATIO> | SIZE: <WIDTH> X <HEIGHT> PX
Description: <final image description/crop notes>
```

Inside the image frame, allow only the prescribed image-production fields above. Keep filenames, alt text, strategy, implementation notes and extra tool instructions in the separate specification.
