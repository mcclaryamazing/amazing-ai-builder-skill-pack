# Final wireframe conversion review

For image descriptions, alt text, captions, filenames and on-image text, apply the customer-facing image-text guidance in `product-image-production.md`. Describe the pictured product or scene naturally and include the seller's product name where relevant. Keep production labels and provenance in seller notes. Treat imagined scenes as illustrations.

Run this gate after the full-copy placeholder wireframe is rendered and normal interaction and visual QA is complete, immediately before delivery for user approval. Judge the actual experience of the buyer and the strength of the sales argument.

## Independent role and knowledge

Assign an independent cold-traffic conversion editor, not the writer, this brief:

> Review as an experienced direct-response editor responsible for persuading a skeptical cold visitor. Challenge whether the page earns attention, makes the buyer want this specific product at its price, and gives them a reason to act now. Look for generic, timid or overqualified passages that turn a promising idea into a feature summary. Report material weaknesses where they exist.

Use the actual audience and traffic stage when they differ from the cold-Meta default. Supply the complete rendered desktop/mobile page, image plan, seller brief, actual offer, any supplied proof, audience, traffic and actual ad when available. Have the reviewer assess the page before reading the writer's rationale or earlier scores, then use the strategy packet to distinguish intentional choices from gaps.

Apply [conversion-copy.md](conversion-copy.md), [listicle-playbook.md](listicle-playbook.md) where relevant and the master-copywriter principles in [dtc-visual-copy-system.md](dtc-visual-copy-system.md). Evaluate existing desire, specificity, a convincing reason why, reading momentum, offer clarity and the next action in the brand's voice. Use the seller's facts as the editorial inputs.

## Review judgment

Use three connected lenses:

- Skeptical buyer: What is offered? Why should I want this product at this price? Can I picture the payoff? Why would I act now, and what hesitation remains?
- Conversion editor: Read the hero as someone arriving from the ad, then skim the headings and inspect the offer. A listicle's visible main headline must say “N Reasons” and promise a specific benefit, situation or decision. Check the named product appears before the reasons, the count matches and every numbered reason adds a distinct buying argument. In the full read, ask where a scene, emotional payoff, product detail, demonstration or offer explanation would make the argument stronger. Preserve repetition that serves a purpose at that point. Cut copy that stalls momentum or preemptively answers a worry the page never raises.
- Execution reviewer: Do the rendered hierarchy, mobile experience, controls and image-production instructions consistently support that argument? Compare actual artifacts rather than approving the spec alone.

Preserve a substantive long-form argument by default. Use the buyer, ad, product and offer to explain any overall short-form choice. Develop underexplained reasons and combine sections with no useful role. Update the headline count after cutting or merging a reason. Ask whether the reader would still want the product without the promotion and whether a promotion-led opening has the stated campaign rationale. Keep material purchase conditions and seller-supplied or approved disclosures clear.

## Customer-facing copy sweep

Inspect the exact rendered artifact on desktop and mobile, including headings, body copy, captions, navigation, CTAs, forms, FAQs/accordions, tooltips, selection states and success/error messages. Inspect customer-accessible labels and alt text too. Check browsing before purchase actions separately from post-click feedback: would setup or preview commentary still belong if checkout were connected tomorrow? FAQs should answer product, offer or actual purchase-term questions, not temporary implementation limitations. Judge purpose and interaction state; "preview" or "demo" can be legitimate product language. For each passage, ask whether it builds desire, explains the product, supports belief, answers an objection, advances the response, or supplies necessary buyer guidance, accessibility or seller-supplied or approved disclosures. Assess both missing depth and unnecessary repetition; preserve material information, useful explanation and purposeful reinforcement. Do not classify a passage as filler solely because its idea appears elsewhere.

Reject seller-facing text such as “this section builds trust,” “place the CTA here,” “conversion strategy,” “insert testimonial” or “confirm shipping terms” anywhere exposed to customers. Move it to the separate specification or seller handoff. Limit image placeholders to their prescribed slot, size, description and crop guidance. Apply the same rule to accessible labels.

Check that the copy preserves the seller's product details, actual offer and approved disclosures. Remove qualifications that do nothing for the decision or fix a misleading implication at its source. Keep essential unanswered offer or setup questions in the seller handoff. For an unconnected purchase action, show truthful immediate feedback such as “Demo only. No order was placed and no payment was taken.” Keep preview status, missing connections and installation instructions in the seller handoff unless a visibly labeled prototype was requested.

Record the inspected artifact, viewports and interaction states, offending passages and fixes, and any inspection limits. Zero remaining seller-facing passages outside prescribed image placeholders is a mandatory acceptance condition. Fix accepted violations and recheck affected rendering/states before delivery; an uninspected surface cannot be marked passed. For copy-only work inspect the supplied scope and disclose that no rendered-page sweep occurred. Reapply this sweep to the implemented page before launch.

## Findings and remediation

For each material finding, give the exact passage or location, the effect on the buying decision and the smallest useful correction. Distinguish concrete defects, editorial opportunities and test ideas. Prioritize changes that make a meaningful difference to the buyer.

The writer evaluates every finding and records acceptance or rejection with reasons. Fix concrete defects and accepted editorial opportunities within the authorized design scope. Re-render and recheck affected copy, controls, layout and image specifications; obtain focused independent verification of consequential fixes. One coordinated round is the default, not a chain of full redesign reviews. Further review is warranted only for unresolved material issues or newly introduced defects.

Resolve accepted findings before delivering the reviewed wireframe. Use the seller's supplied information to strengthen the argument. Ask for a decision when a required offer, selection or action cannot be represented clearly with the information available.

Include a concise review record with the wireframe covering independent participation, important findings, accepted fixes and rechecks, rejected findings with reasons, and any remaining purchase-path decisions or useful test hypotheses. Keep user approval, final-image approval and deployment authorization separate.

If independent-agent capability is unavailable, perform a clearly labeled separate editorial pass using the same brief, disclose that independence was unavailable, and never claim an independent review occurred. Lack of that capability does not justify skipping review or remediation.
