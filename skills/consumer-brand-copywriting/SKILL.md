# Consumer Brand Copywriting

## Core Posture

Write copy like a conversion strategist, not a slogan machine. Start from the customer, the offer, and the proof. Channel existing desire; do not invent desire, claims, testimonials, statistics, certifications, guarantees, pricing, shipping terms, ingredients, specs, or endorsements.

Use the user's facts as the claim boundary:

- State supplied facts confidently.
- Turn supplied features into practical and emotional benefits.
- Mark missing claims as `[CLAIM TO VERIFY: ...]`.
- Mark missing proof/assets as `[PROOF NEEDED: ...]` or `[ASSET NEEDED: ...]`.
- Never borrow claim substance from competitor examples, landing-page exemplars, or the corpus.

For detailed channel patterns and checklists, read `references/consumer-brand-copywriting-framework.md` when the task involves full-page copy, ad copy, product page copy, email sequences, or a copy audit.

## Required Inputs

Before drafting substantive copy, gather or infer from available context:

- Product: what it is, category, differentiators, price, shipping, specs, ingredients/materials, usage.
- Customer: who it is for, pain/desire, buying trigger, objections, awareness level.
- Offer: discount, bundle, subscription, free gift, guarantee, urgency, CTA destination.
- Proof: reviews, ratings, testimonials, studies, press, certifications, customer count, founder story.
- Brand voice: tone words, do/don't rules, terminology, examples.
- Channel and placement: landing page, PDP, Meta ad, email, quiz, social post, article, etc.

If brand voice is missing, propose a compact voice based on audience/category and label it as an assumption.

## Universal Copy Rules

- Lead with the customer outcome, not the product internals.
- Use one big idea per page/ad/email. Do not dilute the offer with unrelated angles.
- Make the first visible copy confirm the visitor is in the right place.
- Prefer clarity over cleverness, especially in headlines, CTAs, and above-the-fold copy.
- Use conversational language, short sentences, direct address, and concrete nouns.
- Translate every feature into a benefit using "so you can..." logic.
- Add emotion after clarity: relief, confidence, ease, pride, control, status, comfort, joy.
- Build believability with real proof. If proof is absent, design a proof slot instead of fabricating.
- Keep copy skimmable: short paragraphs, benefit bullets, descriptive subheads, repeated CTAs.
- Use truthful urgency only. No fake scarcity, fake countdowns, or unsupported "best" claims.

## Landing Page Workflow

For paid landing pages, produce final copy, not vague direction:

1. Define the message match: driving ad hook -> hero headline/subhead -> first CTA.
2. Write a short benefit-driven hero headline, plus 2 alternates.
3. Write a one-sentence subhead that names the audience, product category, and outcome.
4. Use the first CTA to match the offer and destination: `Take the Quiz`, `Shop Now`, `Start Your Plan`, `Add to Cart`, etc.
5. Build sections in persuasive order: problem/desire, benefits, how it works, proof, offer, risk reversal, objections/FAQ, final CTA.
6. Put each section H2 in benefit language, not label language.
7. Include proof where the claim is made, not only at the bottom.
8. Use FAQ to remove buying friction: fit, usage, shipping, returns, safety/compliance, subscription cancellation.
9. Repeat the primary CTA after major persuasion blocks and include a mobile sticky CTA when appropriate.

## Meta Ad To Landing Page Match

For Meta/Facebook ads, write for retrieval first, persuasion second:

- In the first 1-2 sentences, state the problem/desire, product category, and core benefit.
- Avoid metaphor-first or curiosity-only hooks.
- Repeat a natural semantic cluster across primary text, headline, description, and landing-page hero.
- Put story, metaphor, and emotional color after the retrieval-safe opener.
- Use the headline as a clarity anchor: product category + primary benefit.
- Use the description for benefit + proof or benefit + risk reversal.

When designing a landing page from an ad, echo the ad's problem, category, and benefit above the fold. Do not pivot to a new angle.

## Output Standards

For full landing-page specs, include:

- Hero headline and 2 alternates.
- Subhead.
- Primary CTA and microcopy.
- Section-by-section final copy.
- Benefit bullets.
- Proof copy with source labels.
- Offer/risk-reversal copy.
- FAQ questions and answers.
- Missing claim/proof/asset placeholders.
- Notes on message match and voice choices.

For audits, classify issues as:

- Missing customer outcome.
- Feature without benefit.
- Vague or clever-before-clear headline.
- Unsupported claim.
- Weak offer or CTA mismatch.
- Proof too late or too vague.
- Objection not handled.
- Voice inconsistency.
- Poor ad-to-LP message match.

## Relationship To Design Work

When used with `frontend-design`, this skill owns the persuasive language and message architecture. `frontend-design` owns the visual thesis, layout quality, typography, imagery, and interaction feel. Keep them aligned: copy should specify what the visual must prove, and design should make the copy easier to believe.
